Before they were children’s stories, these were court romances of Janggala and Kediri: a prince who leaves the river-mouth, a princess who vanishes inland, and a road along the Brantas that still exists under another name.

Ande-Ande Lumut is the same search, told as a riddle at the door.

01

Ande-Ande Lumut

A widow in a village on the Brantas has four daughters. Three of them are loud with their own importance. The youngest, Klenting Kuning, keeps the hearth and takes the blows. When a handsome stranger called Ande-Ande Lumut sends word that he will marry the girl who can reach his house, the elder sisters set out dressed for a wedding. Klenting Kuning is told to stay and pound rice.

A river blocks the road. Yuyu Kangkang, a giant crab, offers a crossing for a kiss. The three sisters pay. Their faces come up streaked with mud. Klenting Kuning, when she finally follows, refuses. She finds another way, arrives with clean feet, and is the only one Ande-Ande Lumut will receive.

The poor man at the gate is Panji Asmarabangun in disguise. The girl with the mortar is Cakra Kirana, who has also been living under another name. The test is not beauty. It is whether she will sell herself to cross.
02

Panji’s search

In the older court version the lovers are already promised: Raden Inu Kertapati of Jenggala to Dewi Sekartaji of Daha, who is also called Cakra Kirana. War, a storm, or a jealous kinsman pulls them apart. Panji does not wait in the palace. He puts on a commoner’s name and walks.

The walk is not a fantasy geography. Jenggala sat on the eastern delta — its harbour, Hujung Galuh, is the old city of Surabaya. Kediri, the kingdom of Panjalu, sat upriver at Daha. Between them the Brantas makes a corridor through what are now Sidoarjo, Mojokerto, Jombang and Nganjuk. Those towns are later names for the same valley courts a 12th-century party would have had to pass.

He is looking for a person, not a throne. Each court is a question: has she been here, and what did it cost to come this far?

The road to Candra Kirana

Pins sit on present civic centres that occupy the historic ground. Each dashed line is a real road along the Brantas corridor; the number is driving distance in kilometres. Choose a start and an end. The map then runs Dijkstra’s algorithm — it settles the nearest unvisited court, relaxes every road out of it, and keeps the cheapest way it has seen — until it holds the shortest path.
